Deferred revenue vs deferred expenses. By Nicholas Bouché. Deferred revenue is the part of the revenue you invoiced in advance for products or services that are going to be delivered in the future. It is recorded as liabilities when the bill is issued to the customer. Then in each period, you recognize a part of it as revenue recognition and decrease from this amount the liabilities. For example, deferred revenue can be rent payments received in advance, or annual subscription payments received at the beginning of the year. Deferred expenses are costs that have been invoiced from your suppliers but not incurred. These expenses are typically for goods or services that will be used in the future. These expenses are recorded as assets when the bill is issued by the supplier. Then in each period, you recognize a part of it as expenses incurred and decrease from this amount the assets. Deferred expenses can be yearly rent, annual advertising fees, or advance payment of insurance coverage. Follow Nicholas Bouché for more finance tips.
