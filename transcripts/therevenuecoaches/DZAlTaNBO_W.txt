Twenty-five percent of startups don't fail because they can't build a product. They fail because they can't acquire customers fast enough. And here's how it happens: the product ships, the team celebrates, and then someone says, "Well, now we need to get some customers," and that's the first time anyone's really thought about it. Or, "Hey, marketing, what's the plan?" But by the time they figure out who the buyer is, what channel works, and what message lands, the runway is gone. Customer acquisition isn't something you figure out after a product launch. It has to be designed before you spend a single dollar on development. The companies that survive are the ones that treat go-to-market as seriously as the product itself.
