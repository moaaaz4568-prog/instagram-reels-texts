We've worked across companies and helped them generate over a billion dollars in revenue. And the pattern we see in early startups that struggle is almost always the same. It's not the product. The product is usually solid, sometimes even exceptional. But 55% of startup failures comes from a go-to-market problem. And that breaks down into two things we see constantly. First, they've never actually validated a real market need exists. They assumed it, they built for it, but they never confirmed it before going to market. Second, even when the need is real, they move too slowly on customer acquisition. They burn through 12 to 18 months of runway testing and iterating before they close the meaningful number of customers. By then, it's too late to course correct. The product was never the problem, the go-to-market was.
