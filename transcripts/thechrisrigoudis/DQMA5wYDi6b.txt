Sales managers will attack closers after one bad week of sales, even if they've been the top closer for an entire year. If they're a top performer, they're self-aware enough that they already had a bad week. When you're kicking them while they're down, it just creates resentment in the workplace. And instead of themselves feeling bad about their bad performance, you're creating a scenario where instead of the team working together and you're empowering them to win, you are working against each other. If they already showed you that they're a top performer, all you need to do is put them back out there instead of kicking them while they're down.
