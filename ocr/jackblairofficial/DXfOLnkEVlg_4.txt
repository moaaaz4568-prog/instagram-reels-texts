4. "You can go with someone newer and hungrier. I understand the appeal. What I can tell you is what that decision usually looks like twelve months later."

Clay Morrow never apologised for the ground he had covered or the knowledge it had given him.
This phrase acknowledges the competition directly and converts the experience gap into the most compelling argument available.

The buyer who was leaning toward the energetic newcomer suddenly has to think about what energy without experience has cost them before.
You are not attacking the competitor. You are asking the buyer to remember their own history with that choice.
Their own memory is always more persuasive than your argument.

Reference their history with the energetic newcomer. Their experience always closes better than yours.
