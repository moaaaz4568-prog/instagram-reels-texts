1. He absorbed the heat before he steered.

The first sentence after a hot objection decides whether the buyer keeps swinging or starts listening. Thompson called it empathy absorbing tension. I gave Claude my eight ugliest objections and asked for the deflector for each. What came back:

"Too expensive" became "You've been burned on price before and you're not doing it again. Walk me through what you paid last time and what you got for it." Under 25 words each, no "but", and a flag on the two objections where the buyer is only testing whether I'll flinch.
