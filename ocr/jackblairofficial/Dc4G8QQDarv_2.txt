2. "IT IS ABOUT THE PRICE OF A SANDWICH A DAY. TWELVE DOLLARS. CHEAPER THAN THE MISTAKE IT STOPS."
Knowles argued that buyers run a resistance script the moment they hear a price. A small surprise interrupts the script, and the next sentence gets in before it restarts.
Convert the number into a unit the buyer does not expect, then give the real figure, then name what it replaces. The surprise is the wedge. The verdict is the sale.
A price the buyer has to re-hear is a price they have not yet refused.
