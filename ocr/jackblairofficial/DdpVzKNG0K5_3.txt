3. "You're right about the direction. The size is what's off."

In their second study, 2,801 people saw misstatements corrected against the same data, and the average person moved toward the fact regardless of which side the fact had spoken. Concede the half they have right before you touch the half they have wrong. It costs you nothing and it is usually true.

A correction lands at the size of the error, not the size of the person.
