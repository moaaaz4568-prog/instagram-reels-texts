5. "I think you already know this is the right decision. The question is whether you are willing to act on what you know."

This phrase removes the intellectual distance a buyer uses to avoid committing.
Cersei always made the cost of inaction personal rather than theoretical.

The buyer who has been hiding behind process and policy suddenly has nowhere left to stand.
They either act on what they know or they admit they are choosing to stay stuck.
Most buyers will not admit that out loud. The close follows the silence.

Make the cost of not deciding more uncomfortable than the decision itself.
