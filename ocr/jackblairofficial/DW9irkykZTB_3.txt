3. "I want to make sure I understand what you're actually saying."

Not an agreement. Not a pushback.
A pause dressed as a question.

This phrase slows the conversation to your pace.
It forces the buyer to clarify, commit, or expose the real objection.

Rhoades never let a vague answer stand.
Neither should you.
