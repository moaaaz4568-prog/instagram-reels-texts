2. "WHAT WOULD GET IN THE WAY OF DOING THIS AT YOUR PLACE?"
The discussion groups spent most of their time on obstacles. The smell, the family’s reaction, how to cook the thing. Lewin let the objections out in the open where the group could answer them.
Ask for the obstacles before the buyer volunteers them as a no. Named in a group, an objection becomes a problem to solve. Held privately, it becomes the reason.
An objection said out loud is half solved. An objection kept quiet is a decision already made.
